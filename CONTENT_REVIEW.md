# Cultural and educational content: review list

Every statement the prototype makes about Hawaiian language, culture or farming is listed here. It was drafted from general knowledge **and has not yet been checked against sources or with practitioners.** Nothing here should ship until a cultural advisor and an ʻōlelo Hawaiʻi reviewer have signed off.

The text lives in `scripts/main.gd` (`TIPS`, `TUTU_TALK`, `GLOSSARY`, `_end_level`) and in station labels in `scripts/station.gd`.

**Status key:** 🟫 historically documented · 🟩 contemporary practice · 🟨 game simplification · ❓ needs checking

## Claims made in gameplay

| # | What the game says or does | Type | Notes / where to verify |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Water is diverted from the kahawai at a poʻo wai into an ʻauwai, flows through loʻi terrace to terrace, and returns to the kahawai | 🟫🟩 | Handy & Handy, *Native Planters in Old Hawaii*; living loʻi practitioners. Check the term *poʻo wai* and how it is used. |
| 2 | Kalo is replanted from huli (the top of the harvested plant) | 🟫🟩 | Widely documented; confirm wording with a mahiʻai. |
| 3 | Kalo grows best in cool, flowing water; still, warm water can cause rot | 🟩 | Matches modern agronomy (root rot in warm, stagnant water). Confirm framing with farmers. |
| 4 | Taking all the stream's water harms ʻoʻopu and downstream users | 🟩 | Reflects modern instream-flow issues and native stream ecology. Decide with advisors how much history and politics to include. |
| 5 | Raw kalo (corm and leaves) is itchy (maneʻo) and must be cooked | 🟫 | Calcium oxalate. Confirm *maneʻo* is the right word. |
| 6 | The imu cooks with hot stones under leaves and earth | 🟫🟩 | Simplified: no stone heating step in the game. |
| 7 | Paʻiʻai is pounded cooked kalo; poi is paʻiʻai thinned with water | 🟫 | Pounding in the game uses water from the stand (wetting the board and pounder). Confirm. |
| 8 | Paʻiʻai was wrapped in lāʻī (ti leaves) for keeping and travel | 🟫 | Confirm. |
| 9 | Lūʻau = cooked young kalo leaves | 🟫 | Confirm. The modern word *lūʻau* for a feast came later, so the game uses *ʻaha ʻāina* for the feast. Check that choice. |
| 10 | Floods: closing the poʻo wai protects the kuāuna | 🟨 | Plausible but simplified. Ask how flood protection was actually done. |
| 11 | Weeds stop growth until pulled | 🟨 | Simplified. |
| 12 | Time is heavily compressed (kalo matures in about 40 seconds, versus many months in reality) | 🟨 | Should be stated in-game for learners. Not yet done. |
| 13 | Growing is shown as a farmer's job for anyone | 🟨 | Under the ʻai kapu, some roles (for example pounding poi) were gendered. Decide with advisors whether and how to address this. |

## Words used (`GLOSSARY` and labels)

ʻāina, ahupuaʻa, ʻaha ʻāina, ʻauwai, huli, imu, ipu, kahawai, kalo, kī / lāʻī, kuāuna, lau, loʻi, lūʻau, mahiʻai, mauka / makai, maneʻo, mauʻu, ʻōpala, ʻoʻopu, paʻiʻai, papa kuʻi ʻai, pōhaku kuʻi ʻai, poi, poʻo wai, ʻumeke, wai, ʻono, ʻohana, Tūtū, kūpuna, Pau hana, Aloha mai.

- Check spelling, ʻokina and kahakō against Pukui & Elbert, *Hawaiian Dictionary* (wehewehe.org).
- **The game deliberately avoids building Hawaiian phrases.** Labels mix single Hawaiian words with English, such as "Paʻiʻai in lāʻī". A language reviewer may want to replace these with proper Hawaiian phrasing.
- "Pau hana" on the end screen is everyday local usage. Confirm it fits.

## Representation choices to discuss

- Tūtū is a present-day kupuna NPC. Decide whose voice she speaks in, whether to voice-act her, and in which dialect.
- Placeholder characters have no specific identity. Final art should be developed with community input.
- The level is not modelled on a specific place. If it becomes one (a specific ahupuaʻa), get that community's permission and input.
